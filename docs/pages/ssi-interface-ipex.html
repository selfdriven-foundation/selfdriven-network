The premise

Nothing is disclosed until both sides have agreed to the terms.

A credential holds attributes that belong to someone. IPEX lets the discloser put the terms first: the offer carries the credential with its rules in full, and its attribute values only as a digest.

The disclosee accepts by signing an agree that cites the offer. Only then does the grant reveal the values, and the digest proves they are the ones that were offered.

A conversation that can’t be rewritten

Each exn carries, in p, the SAID of the message it answers. Drop, reorder or swap a message and the chain no longer reads.

      The protocol

      Six routes, one conversation.

      Every IPEX message is an exn under /ipex. Three routes can open an exchange; the rest must cite the message they answer, and only certain replies are allowed.

      1. 01 · apply

        Ask for a credential

        Disclosee. Can open. Names the schema SAID and the attribute labels it wants.

      2. 02 · offer

        Set out the terms

        Discloser. Can open, or answers apply. Embeds the credential with its attribute block compacted to a SAID.

      3. 03 · agree

        Accept the terms

        Disclosee. Answers offer. Its signature binds the disclosee to the rules it was shown.

      4. 04 · grant

        Disclose

        Discloser. Can open, or answers agree. Embeds the full credential, its TEL iss event and the KEL event that anchors it.

      5. 05 · admit

        Accept the credential

        Disclosee. Answers grant, once its agent has verified what arrived.

      6. 06 · spurn

        Decline

        Either side. Answers apply, offer, agree or grant, and closes the exchange.

      Methods

      One method per route.

      Each call returns the signed exn and its CESR attachments, ready for the counterparty. The Interface call tab on every message in the transcript shows the request and response for it.

      Hosted identifiers sign inside the interface. A self-hosted agent can sign locally and post the finished message instead.

      • Disclosee

        ssi-ipex-apply

        sender, recipient, schema, attributes, message.

      • Discloser

        ssi-ipex-offer

        sender, recipient, credential, prior (an apply, optional), message.

      • Disclosee

        ssi-ipex-agree

        sender, prior (the offer), message.

      • Discloser

        ssi-ipex-grant

        sender, recipient, credential, prior (an agree, optional), message.

      • Disclosee

        ssi-ipex-admit

        sender, prior (the grant), message. Verifies the grant before it signs.

      • Either side

        ssi-ipex-spurn

        sender, prior, message.

      Fine print

      What is real here, and what is not.

      Everything runs in this page. Nothing is sent anywhere.

      • 01

        Real

        Blake3-256 SAIDs, Ed25519 signatures (RFC 8032) and CESR encoding, in plain JavaScript. A run of this page was checked in KERIpy 1.3.6: both key event logs were accepted, every SAID and signature verified, and KERIpy’s Exchanger parsed the grant.

      • 02

        Simplified

        No witnesses, no key rotation and no transport. Messages pass between identifiers inside the page, not over OOBIs and mailboxes. Keys live in memory and are gone when you reload.

      • 03

        Disclosure

        The grant discloses the whole attribute block. Selective disclosure, with attributes split into separately blinded blocks, is beyond this page. The salts in u keep the sealed values from being guessed.

      • 04

        Compatibility

        KERIpy computes a credential’s SAID over its full form. The ToIP ACDC draft computes it over the most compact form, which would let an offer be verified on its own. This page follows KERIpy. It also sets rp on every message, where KERIpy’s builders leave it empty.
