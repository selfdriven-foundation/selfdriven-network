# 01 — Purpose and framing

## What the kit is

The **selfdriven.network Silicon Kit** is a practical proposal for linking physical systems to a community-oriented network. It starts with evidence generated by a device, then adds community-controlled rules, identity and context, before any intelligent or economic action is permitted.

The aim is **community self-actuation**, not another general-purpose chip or proprietary platform. It should allow an individual, a cooperative or an independent Octo to work with real-world infrastructure while retaining meaningful human accountability.

Three separations matter:

1. **Silicon supplies evidence**: a protected key may sign a statement; counters and secure boot may strengthen device provenance.
2. **Network supplies interpretation**: entities, contexts, permissions, connections, evidence policy and coordination reside in network interfaces and data.
3. **Communities supply legitimacy**: who is allowed to assert something, how to challenge it, and what consequences follow are governance choices.

## What the kit is not

This is not a claim that the Foundation has fabricated an ASIC, that an experimental signer is tamper-proof, or that a local policy file is a valid credential. It is not a system for turning signed claims directly into payments, licences or safety actions. The proposed interfaces here are not represented as deployed APIs.

## Outcomes

- A portable **hardware-to-network evidence contract** independent of a single chip or blockchain.
- Community enrolment, suspension, key rotation and revocation as first-class concerns.
- Data minimisation: share a proof or derived fact when raw data is unnecessary.
- Offline-first capture with later verification and sync, where suitable.
- Clear assurance distinctions: software key, protected secure element, attested firmware, validated sensor, corroborated event.
- Independent verification of evidence and an inspectable chain of reasoning for community decisions.

## 4Cs applied to silicon

**Curious**: publish explanations and experiments. **Caring**: avoid mass telemetry and unconsented identifiers. **Constructive**: produce concrete interoperable artefacts. **Chill**: make entry possible with a laptop before a chip foundry.

## Primary audiences

Community infrastructure operators, makers, educators, governance designers, open-silicon collaborators, interface maintainers, and developers working on Octonomous, selfdrivenOS and physical-world verification.

## First question

What physical activity would a community genuinely benefit from verifying — and which facts would remain uncertain even after cryptographic verification?
